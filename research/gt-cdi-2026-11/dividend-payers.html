Regular Dividend Payers

Hold the Brazilian stocks that paid a dividend in each of the last three years and currently have the highest 12-month yield. Rebalance twice a year. Compare everything against CDI after Brazilian taxes.

Verdict: rejected It looked promising in three separate test periods, then failed the readiness tests: it trailed the DIVO11 dividend ETF and showed no reliable edge over the market. See the readiness tests.

Research findings from historical backtests, not investment advice. Every number on this page comes from Quantax Bench runs #31–#51, each starting with R$100k and no contributions.

How it picks stocks

  1. Start from liquid B3 stocks.Average daily traded value of at least R$2 million and a price of at least R$1. Share classes of the same company (PETR3 / PETR4) count once.
  2. Keep regular payers only.The company paid a cash dividend or JCP in each of the last three 12-month windows (div_years_3y >= 3).
  3. Rank by dividend yield.Trailing 12-month dividends and JCP per share, each payment divided by the price on the day it was paid (div_yield_12m).
  4. Buy the top 20%, equal weight.Every selected stock gets the same share of the portfolio.
  5. Rebalance in June and December only.Between those months the portfolio drifts with no trades and no tax. The engine builds the list from data up to the previous period, so it never trades on information it couldn't have had.

Trading is light: about 2.4 times the portfolio's value bought and sold per year, the lowest of the strategies where I measured it. Sales only happen in June and December, so tax is due only in those two months; at R$100k those sales usually exceed the R$20k monthly exemption.

Why it might work

The idea is that a tax benefit and a governance signal both go unpriced, because the investors who set prices don't care about either.

The government

Dividends are tax-free for Brazilian residents, while CDI interest is taxed. Part of a dividend stock's return reaches a local investor untaxed.

The price-setters

Foreign investors (a large share of B3 trading) and tax-exempt funds get nothing from that exemption, so they don't pay up for it.

Controlling shareholders

They can extract value from minority holders. Paying dividends every year is a costly commitment not to.

Who pays you

Holders of non-payers, who bear that extraction risk, and price-setters who leave the tax benefit on the table.

The engine taxes dividends as capital gains when a stock is sold, so the tax advantage is not credited in these results. The real after-tax figures would be somewhat better.

Warning signs before the readiness tests

  • It doesn't beat the index. Over the same months, holding BOVA11 matched it from 2015 to 2026 and did better from 2020.
  • The final test passed partly by luck of timing. The run's first rebalance was 31 March 2020, so it skipped the COVID crash. That came from the quarterly schedule, not from anything the strategy chose.
  • It is mostly market exposure. It beat CDI in every up year and lost to it in 9 of 10 down years, though it fell less than the broad market in 2008 (−25.6% vs −48.8%).
  • The statistics are weak. It was the best of 18 strategy variants tried, and no t-statistic reaches 2.
  • The final test wasn't clean. I had already seen 2020–2026 results from earlier tests, and I knew that large dividend payers like Petrobras and the banks did well in 2021–2023.
  • Drawdowns are deep. It fell about 42% from peak during the 2015–early 2016 sell-off.

What was planned to settle it (now done, see readiness tests)

  • Compare a 50/50 mix of this strategy and CDI against a 50/50 mix of BOVA11 and CDI, to separate stock picking from market exposure.
  • Test it on data after 2026, which nobody has looked at.
  • Model the dividend tax exemption, and compare against CDI after its own 15% income tax, which is what an individual actually earns.

These used one continuous run from 2005 to 2026 instead of three separate ones, and that changed the picture. As the account grows, the R$20k monthly exemption covers less of each sale and taxes rise (R$182k paid in total). The run also took the March 2020 crash (−29.5% that month). Over 2006–2026, two of the three rebalance schedules ended with less money than CDI would have paid, and the largest fall from peak was about 60%.

The strategy moves about 0.76 as much as the market and adds no return that can be told apart from noise. A dividend ETF did the same job better.
